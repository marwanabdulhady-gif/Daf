# The cast of the seventeen doors

**Companion to `00-epic-architecture.md`.** This is the authoring contract for
every character that appears in a ribbon, a canvas, a handoff or a boss screen.
Rules from the epic architecture apply: the fiction lane is labelled fictional;
the documented-account treatment is reserved for STEM windows; the Amanah lane
is separate and never gamified; Omar and Zayd **never resemble or represent the
Prophet Muhammad ﷺ, his companions or any historical person** (existing
`visualPolicy`, restated because it is non-negotiable).

---

## 1 · Omar — the keeper of evidence

**Role in the fellowship:** the Scribe by nature.
**One line:** *He trusts only what is written — and he is afraid the ink will dry before he is ready.*

- **Flaw:** commitment. Omar records, questions, hedges. The "ink is still
  wet" anxiety: signing a number before it is checked feels like danger. In
  door 1 his seal is earned *for* the class; he does not yet dare to sign
  first.
- **Desire (private, never stated as dialogue to a sacred figure — this is a
  fictional boy's want):** to be the one the council believes without him
  having to show the work twice.
- **Arc:** from *is the evidence enough?* (door 1) to *I can tell what counts
  as evidence — and I will teach it to the next class* (door 17). By door 13 he
  signs the challenge to the Muddler **before** seeing the full proof, because
  he trusts the class's method. After the boss he stops double-checking the
  same line twice and starts checking the *next* line instead.
- **Voice:** short, exact, question-shaped. He says "show me" and "which
  piece?" He never says "I think"; he says "the record says" — until door 17,
  when, in the letter forward, he writes his first "I am sure, and here is why."

### Omar's door beats (one per quest — write ribbon copy from these)

| Door | Beat |

## 2 · Zayd — the builder of models

**Role in the fellowship:** the Builder by nature.
**One line:** *He builds before he checks — and the things he builds are almost right, which is harder to fix than the wrong ones.*

- **Flaw:** sequence. Zayd starts the build and calls it done at the same
  moment; checking is an interruption. His mistakes are *productive* (the
  existing character file says so, and the story honours it): every one of his
  jammed lifts and missing regions becomes a lesson the class keeps.
- **Desire:** to build something no one has to check. (The arc shows him
  learning that checked is what makes it buildable.)
- **Arc:** from *it works!* (door 1) to *it works — here is the proof, and
  here is how to check it again* (door 17). By door 13 he is the one who
  builds the class's defence of the Muddler's strike cards *while* Omar signs
  for it. After the boss his habit flips: he says "let us test" before "let us
  try."
- **Voice:** fast, demonstrative, shows not tells. He says "watch this" and
  "one more piece." His errors land as *the lift is jammed*, *the line is
  missing*, *the corner is tiny* — concrete, repairable, never shameful.

### Zayd's door beats

| Door | Beat |
|---|---|
| 1 · the census | He tries to fill the torn page from memory of the pattern. The class makes him fill it from the *places*, and he sees the pattern was the places all along. |
| 2 · the ledger | He builds the compensation bar and moves the wrong riyal across (gives without taking). The total bar lies to him. He builds the trade rule himself, slowly, and keeps the scar of that bar in the folio. |
| 3 · the grove | His cut comes first and his check comes last — the foreman (3-2) makes him estimate before cutting. He wins the estimate by a margin and does not trust his own win. |
| 4 · the workshop | **The jammed lift (4-7)** is his signature moment: his obvious method fails midway; he has to revise without guessing. He stops the day saying "failure was the middle step talking." |
| 5 · the caravan | He wants to throw the remainder away (5-4); the passengers left at the gate make him keep it. He builds three different endings for one division and labels all three honest. |
| 6 · the council | He builds a plan that is right but cannot be defended; the council asks for the middle answer and he rebuilds *with* it shown. First time he designs for the checker, not just the builder. |
| 7 · the reef | He finds every rectangle but keeps missing the prime ones (7-4) because "only one shape" feels like a loss. The class reframes: the indivisible tile is the strongest border there is. |
| 8 · the islands | He builds the bridge markers (8-2) and, when two labels land on one point, he calls it an error. It is the year's quietest miracle: different names, same place. He keeps a "same place" card in the folio. |
| 9 · the kitchen | He mixes tray sizes for speed (9-3) and the portions stop matching. The fixed tray is the model; he rebuilds every tray of the unit at the same size on purpose. |
| 10 · the clockmaker | His repeated batches (10-2) drift against the clock. He builds the schedule with the time *inside* the model instead of beside it. |
| 11 · the diver's board | He draws the line plot that misleads (11-4) — a stack of marks that looks like a story the data doesn't tell. He redoes it. The redone plot goes in the folio next to the false one, labelled. |
| 12 · the docks | He reads the decimal moorings (12-2) by label length and moors the wrong boat; the number line corrects him. He starts writing *value* beside every label he reads. |
| 13 · the measure market | **The confrontation.** He builds the class's strike cards — each one a conversion drawn to scale — while Omar signs for them. When the Muddler heals on a claim that sounds right, it is Zayd who says "a sound claim is not a true one." |
| 14 · the pattern | He builds the rule machine (14-2) and feeds it his own favourite mistake; the machine spits back the rule that would have caught it. He laughs for the first time in the tale at his own error. |
| 15 · the observatory | He sets the shade arm (15-4) by eye, then by wedges, then by protractor — and the three agree. "Three tools, one turn" becomes his saying. |
| 16 · the garden | He draws the missing half of the symmetrical panel (16-5) and the mirror test accepts it. He gives the panel to the exhibition without being asked. |
| 17 · the grand gate | He builds the gate's final volume model (17-10) and, for the first time, builds *slowly* — checking as he goes. The letter forward takes his hand for the last line. |

### The partnership (how the two develop together)

- **Act I — opposite poles.** Omar says "show me the record"; Zayd says "watch
  this." Their CompareConnect-style scenes (the Hassan/Musa pattern already in
  the decks) are written so each is right and each is incomplete.
- **Act II — crossing.** Each steals one habit from the other per act: Omar
  takes *build first, check second* in a controlled way (doors 7–8); Zayd
  takes *record first* in a controlled way (doors 9–10); they both lose once
  each (Omar's false "unequal islands," Zayd's "longer label") — and each loss
  is caught by the other, on camera, in the ribbon copy.
- **Act III — the swap is visible.** At the boss (13) they trade jobs: Zayd
  builds the defence, Omar signs the challenge. After the boss, their catch
  phrases exchange: Omar says "let us test it"; Zayd says "show me the piece."
- **Act IV — the handover.** The letter forward is co-written: Omar's certainty,
  Zayd's proof. The final shot (final screen of 17) is the two of them at the
  Grand Gate, lantern between them, fog at the margins, door open.

## 3 · The lantern and the council

**The Lantern of Numeria** — object, frame narrator's voice. It glows, shows,
keeps the margins. It never speaks dialogue; its "voice" is the tale-open and
tale-close lines (written in the ribbon, ≤90s/≤45s). Practical mapping: the
projector.

**The Council of Numeria** — the quest-givers. On screen the council is always
an *object or a place* (the ledger table, the seal, the council board), never a
portrait of a real person; in the classroom the teacher voices the council in
the quest openers. The council never gives the answer; it gives the *question*
(the unit's driving question, already in the story map) and the *stakes* (the
artifact at risk).

## 5 · The Muddler and the minions of confusion

**The Muddler of the Measure Market** — the fog of confusion.

- **Nature:** a stain in the margins of the damaged journal that learned to
  move numbers. Digits shifted, sums swapped, units crossed. He is a property
  with a face, not a person; he is labelled fictional on screen.
- **Motive:** none that is malice. Confusion is his nature — where a number is
  checked, his ground thins. (This is why only reasoning defeats him: a checked
  number is a place he cannot stand.)
- **Weakness (the boss-13 rule, epic-wide):** he is struck when the class
  *proves* an answer; he heals when the class accepts a claim that merely
  *sounds* right. No speed damage, no brute force.
- **Four forms (the acts):**
  1. **The stain** (Act I): water damage, missing digits, silent zeros. The
     class finds his traces; no face, no name — only "the fog."
  2. **The whisper** (Act II): a beautiful but false claim inside confident
     reports; a longer label; two islands too close to judge. Still no face.
  3. **The fog** (Act III, door 13): his market, his face, the great
     confrontation (boss-13, already built). After the boss: the market
     reopens, the fog thins.
  4. **The wisp** (Act IV): a thin wisp at the folio's margins. The letter
     forward names him plainly and warns: *the fog returns in new units* —
     decimals, unlike denominators, volume. He is remembered, not gone.
- **Voice (doors 13–17 only):** the Muddler speaks in the boss and, after the
  boss, in exactly one closing line per quest — always a *claim that sounds
  right* (his healing mechanic in narrative form). Before door 13, traps are
  narrated as *the fog whispers: …* (student-facing phrasing from the lesson's
  `rail.misconception`), never in his voice.

## 6 · Visual and interaction spec

### 6.1 Omar and Zayd — costume, props, poses per act

| Act | Omar | Zayd |
|---|---|---|
| I · the calling | Hall archivist's tunic, **quill** in hand, magnifier at belt; hunched posture. | Work apron over school clothes, **building blocks** in the satchel, hammer strap; forward-leaning posture. |
| II · the journey | Tunic + travel sash, **the folio** now open on his arm (it grows heavier act by act); posture straightening. | Apron + **string of blocks** worn like a belt (they clink when he builds); posture steadying. |
| III · the confrontation | Folio closed, **the seal** at his chest; standing tall. | **Compass** at belt (the observatory's gift), protractor in hand; square-shouldered. |
| IV · the return | Quill returned to his hand *alongside* the seal (he writes and signs again); standing tall, relaxed. | Compass in one hand, a **completed panel** under the other; relaxed, steady. |

**Pose vocabulary** (existing: `question`, `build`, `present`; added this
year, in this order of introduction): `doubt` (door 8 — first used on the
islands), `decide` (door 9), `defend` (door 13, the boss), `celebrate` (door
17, the gate). Each new pose is introduced *because the plot earned it*; a pose
never appears before its door.

**Growth in the art (the rule of visible, not told):**
- Scale: both start at the same small scale inside the ribbon; by Act IV they
  stand at full ribbon height. The Muddler's stain starts full-ribbon and
  shrinks to a corner wisp by Act IV (the villain's art is the mirror of the
  boys' art).
- Palette: their ribbon line-cool the year begins in the existing ink-on-paper
  stage (deep teal, gold); Omar's accent shifts from cool blue (record) to gold
  (signing) across the acts; Zayd's from block-orange (building) to teal
  (checking). The stage itself is unchanged — the growth is in the figures.
- Props are *evidence*, not decoration: every prop Omar/Zayd holds in a ribbon
  is a prop the lesson's math uses (the quill writes the ledger line; the
  compass sets the shade arm). No prop appears that the mission doesn't use.

### 6.2 The Muddler's art (doors 13–17 + the stain/whisper fogs)

- **The stain:** irregular water-darkening at the folio's margins (existing
  damaged-folio look); it *moves* a digit on the tale-open screen (≤90s).
- **The whisper:** no art — a line of ribbon copy in italic, ink-colour, with
  the existing "fog" icon (a single wisp, gold-outlined, never red).
- **The fog (boss-13):** as built — the fog-creature with HP bar; keep the
  existing silhouette, add the market background (the Measure Market's stalls:
  the banner order, the unmarked jugs, the two-scale balance — the door 13
  lesson objects already on the canvases).
- **The wisp:** a thin gold-outlined line at the folio's margin, one breath per
  quest closer. Reduced-motion: the wisp is static.

### 6.3 The Map of the Doors (visual)

Light stage; 17 door icons in four act-rows; gold frame per act (the existing
breathing-frame style); stamped lessons as gold pips inside a door; fog cover
on door 13's icon until the boss (the wisp icon, static in reduced motion);
the opened Grand Gate (door 17) rendered as a gate, not a door — the only
opening that leads *out*.

### 6.4 Interaction rules (what may touch the story)

- The ribbon (StoryShell) is **display-only**; all interaction stays in the
  math components (existing rule: the dark screens are evidence and synthesis,
  never the scored interaction).
- Fellowship roles are teacher-set, rotating per quest; no student is bound to
  a role for the year.
- The Map of the Doors is read-mostly: one tap in, one tap out; the physical
  poster is the class's writable version (ceremony).
- The Muddler's strike cards (boss pattern) are the only story object that is
  *scored*; the score is the class's proof, not a student's race (boss-13 rule).
