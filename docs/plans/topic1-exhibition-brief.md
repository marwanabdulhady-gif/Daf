# Topic 1 exhibition brief · "The census in the brass lantern"

**Unit:** Generalize Place Value Understanding (lessons 1-1…1-5)
**Artifact:** a restored census folio and place-value key
**Public product:** a one-page **population and services brief** for the fictional city of Numeria,
presented by teams to another class (or the class, in one session).

## What the exhibition is

The class finished Topic 1 by restoring a damaged city census, digit by digit, through five
lessons. The brief is the unit's public product: the audience sees the **finished census folio**
(population line, place-value key, comparison record, estimate sheet) and hears **one argument**
delivered in the four-part structure the Sijill named — claim, evidence, reason, conclusion.

## What teams must show (the audience asks for evidence, not story trivia)

1. **The restored population line** — 4,697,000 (simulated), read aloud by periods and written in
   expanded form. *Evidence: the place-value key.*
2. **The ten-times relationship** — one digit, two places, and why the values differ.
   *Evidence: the staircase model or a written ×10 / ÷10 check.*
3. **The comparison record** — the clinic placement: which district is bigger and the place that
   decided it. *Evidence: the first-different-place scan, checked on the number line.*
4. **The estimate sheet** — the supply order: an exact count rounded to the requested place with
   its halfway check. *Evidence: the halfway point of the requested place.*
5. **The argument** — the sealed census line: which reading is supported and the deciding
   evidence, including the 18,000 gap. *Evidence: claim + evidence + reason + conclusion.*

## Rubric anchors (from the plan page)

- **Mathematics (40%)** — accurate Grade 4 content, labelled representations, the five records
  above all present and correct.
- **Reasoning (25%)** — the argument defended with evidence; at least one misconception from the
  unit (zero as nothing, digit vs digit-value, digit-count vs magnitude, add-a-zero) addressed.
- **STEM process (20%)** — the restored folio shows the ask → model/test → compare → revise path.
- **Communication (15%)** — the brief is one page, readable aloud in about three minutes, and the
  audience is asked for the evidence, not the story.

## Ground rules for the audience

- The audience may ask "where is the evidence?" for any claim — that is the unit's question.
- Simulated data stays simulated: the brief labels every number as simulated planning data.
- No team presents the lantern or the boys as part of the evidence; the folio is the exhibit.
