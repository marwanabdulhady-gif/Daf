## Student-facing wording

- **Three new STEM windows** (H1–H3) with cautious wording drafts:
  - *t2-ledgers-that-paper-made* (2-8): "Paper made reliable records possible. In the Islamic
    world, paper spread from Samarkand and Baghdad through the markets of the medieval world, and
    accounts, ledgers and bills were written on it. Positional numerals made those accounts easy to
    check: move a digit and the record changes, so an error shows itself."
  - *t3-rows-that-raise-water* (3-3): "In a treatise completed in 1206, the engineer al-Jazari
    described machines that raised water by repeating one simple action: pot after pot, row after
    row. His designs built on earlier ones, including the work of the Banu Musa brothers.
    Repetition, arranged well, does the work of many."
  - *t4-small-actions-make-machines* (4-7): "The Banu Musa brothers described about a hundred
    devices in their Book of Ingenious Devices, around 850 — fountains, lamps and lifting machines,
    some with controls that acted by themselves. Centuries later, al-Jazari described workshop
    machines that turned and lifted with gears and cranks. Big machines are built from small,
    understood actions — just as a big product is built from smaller multiplications."
- **No Seerah student-facing wording is approved.** The three unit windows remain drafts in
  review packets only; the registry keeps `studentText: null`.
- All scenario numbers (souq riyals, grove rows, panel counts) are simulated and labelled.

## Mathematical verification

- **Topic 2 (4.NBT.B.4, MP.2/3):** ledger totals re-checked as sums of the same addends in
  different orders (commutativity/associativity as audit evidence); subtraction answers verified
  by adding back (difference + subtrahend = minuend); the across-zero screen uses a documented
  ungrouping chain (1 hundred → 10 tens → 10 ones) verified step by step.
- **Topic 3 (4.NBT.B.5):** array totals verified three ways (count, repeated addition, one-digit
  × place value); ×10/×100/×1000 products verified by place shifting (23 → 230 as 23 tens);
  partial products verified by distributive decomposition.
- **Topic 4 (4.NBT.B.5):** 2-digit × 2-digit products verified by area-model partition
  (36×28 = 36×20 + 36×8 = 720 + 288 = 1,008) and by the standard algorithm with the tens-place
  label on the partial product; compatible-number estimates verified within one place-value band
  of the exact product.
- No new standards: `curriculum.json` standards and IXL codes for all 21 lessons are unchanged.
