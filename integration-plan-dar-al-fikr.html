DAR AL FIKR SCHOOLS · IMPLEMENTATION PLAN

Integrating the motivation system into the 114 lessons

What to add, what to draw, what to build, and in what order — mapped onto the code that already exists.

The fact that makes this cheap

All 114 lessons are built from the same ten routine components in one 394-line file. I checked: 114 of 114 lessons use ShowWhatYouKnow, and 114 of 114 use CompareConnect.

So a mechanic added to engine/routines.jsx once inherits to every lesson in the course automatically. The engine work is small and central. The only per-lesson work is writing content — and that is one hard question per lesson, nothing more.

Why the ledger must not live in the student deck

The decks are opened as file:// pages. Browsers treat that origin inconsistently — Chrome shares localStorage across local files, Safari blocks it outright. Cross-lesson student state would be unreliable and would silently vanish on a different device. The Sijill and the budget are class-level, teacher-owned data anyway. Putting them in one console file the teacher runs removes the sync problem entirely and is simpler to build.

2 · The seven mechanics — element, visual, tool

1The Sijill — named-methods ledger

Every deck already ends its Connect screen with two fictional named strategies — "Salma's way", "Ziad's way". The mechanic is to let the teacher overwrite the fictional name with the real student who produced that method in the room, and keep it for the year.

AddWhatWhere
ELEMENT A "This was ______'s method" field on each of the two Connect panels. Writes {topic, lesson, method, student} to the console. routines.jsx → CompareConnect
VISUAL Sijill card — a gold-bordered slip showing the method name, the student, and the lesson it came from. Reuses the existing Star8 mark. new component in routines.jsx
VISUAL Sijill modal — press S in any deck to see every named method so far this year, grouped by topic. app.jsx, beside the existing Overview / Rail / Math modals
PRINT Sijill wall card — A5, one per named method, goes on the classroom wall. This is the mechanic that actually works; the screen version is the backup. print/sijill-card.html
2The hard lane

The single most important mechanic. Each Show What You Know gains an optional second question that is visibly harder. Attempting it is logged whether or not it is answered correctly; only the hard lane can earn a Sijill naming.

AddWhatWhere
ELEMENT New hard prop on ShowWhatYouKnow: {prompt, omml, options, right, support}. Same shape as the existing question, so nothing new to learn. routines.jsx → ShowWhatYouKnow
VISUAL Two doors. Before the question appears: two panels — Standard and Harder — the harder one marked with a gold chevron. Choosing is public and deliberate. No default, no skip. new component LaneChoice
VISUAL Attempt ribbon. A gold ribbon on the Close screen reading "took the harder question" — shown for an attempt, not a success. This is the inversion; it must be visible. routines.jsx → Closing
CONTENT 114 hard questions, one per lesson. Roughly 2–3 hours of authoring per topic. This is the bulk of the real work. lessons/*.jsx
3Commit before you see

Half-built already. LaunchEstimate has Lock my estimate; CardSort has no grading until the class commits. Extend the same idea to the two Monitor screens in every lesson.

AddWhatWhere
ELEMENT commit prop on ExploreChips: student picks a chip, presses Call it, and only then does the canvas animate the model. routines.jsx → ExploreChips
VISUAL Commit bar — a teal bar under the chips that fills and locks. Once locked it shows the prediction next to the result, so the student sees their own thinking beside the answer. new component CommitBar
CONTENT None. The chips already exist in all 114 lessons. —
4A shared budget that carries across weeks

Replaces the fake "catastrophic events". The class holds one resource — riyals for a real thing they chose — that persists between lessons. A poor collective decision means next week starts poorer.

AddWhatWhere
ELEMENT Budget lives in the console. The deck shows it read-only and can post a spend event at the Close screen. console.html + app.jsx footer
VISUAL Budget strip — spent / committed / remaining. The existing D.strip() canvas tool already draws exactly this, including the dashed "unknown" segment and the total brace. NO NEW TOOL draw.js → strip (exists)
CONTENT One decision per week that the class actually votes on. Written by the teacher, not scripted in the deck. teacher
5Table scarcity

Mostly a paper mechanic, and stronger for it. Each table gets fewer resources than it needs — three "ask the teacher" tokens per lesson, one protractor between four, a limited set of number cards. Effort becomes owed to peers.

AddWhatWhere
PRINT Token sheet — A4, 32 tokens, Dar Al Fikr eight-point star, cut and laminate once. Costs almost nothing and does most of the work. print/tokens.html
VISUAL tokenRow() — new canvas tool. A row of star tokens, spent ones drawn hollow. Used on the console so the room can see what is left. NEW TOOL draw.js
TEACHING The rule has to be held. A teacher who answers a question without taking a token destroys the mechanic in one lesson. training
6Delete every participation point

The subtraction, and the easiest change to make. There are 227 award() calls in lessons/ and 7 in the routines. Almost all are a flat 25 points for clicking a chip.

AddWhatWhere
REMOVE All 227 lesson-level award(...) calls, by script — they follow one pattern, so this is a regex pass plus a verify run, not a manual edit. lessons/*.jsx
REMOVE XPBar and Badges from the header. The four badges currently all unlock in the first ninety seconds; they are noise. app.jsx → Header
REPLACE WITH Two scarce numbers in the header instead: Sijill entries and hard lanes taken. Both are hard to get, so both mean something. app.jsx
7Exhibition at the end of each topic

One artefact per topic, shown to someone who is not the teacher. This is simultaneously the motivation mechanic and the parent-politics solution.

AddWhatWhere
PRINT Exhibition poster template — A3. The question, the method, the named student, the answer, and what it is used for. print/exhibition.html
PRINT Parent standards sheet — generated from the console: which enVision standards were covered, unaided in-class accuracy, Sijill entries for that child. This is what makes the system look rigorous rather than like playing. console.html → print view
CONTENT 17 exhibition briefs — one per topic. Real school problems: paint for the art room, is the canteen queue fair, how much shade does the yard need. teacher + me

4 · What one lesson looks like afterwards

Lesson 13-1, Equivalence with Customary Units of Length — the pilot lesson. Same ten screens. Same maths. Different economy.

Now

  1. Notice & Wonder — +10 per click
  2. Launch estimate — +15 to lock
  3. Fit inches into a foot — +25 to click
  4. Build the table — +25 to click
  5. Card sort — +25 to commit
  6. Two named methods — +10 to switch tab
  7. Board
  8. The rule — +20 to tap
  9. SWYK: 5 feet = ? inches — +50
  10. Close — "170 XP", then reset to zero

Every reward is for touching the screen. All four badges were earned in lesson 1-1 in September.

After

  1. Notice & Wonder — no reward, no change
  2. Launch estimate — lock stays; no points
  3. Call it before the bar animates
  4. Call it before the table fills
  5. Card sort — class commits (unchanged)
  6. Two methods → "This was ______'s method" → Sijill
  7. Board
  8. The rule
  9. Two doors. Standard: 5 feet = ? inches. Harder: the banner ribbon is 2 yards 1 foot — how many inches?
  10. Close — ribbon for taking the hard lane, Sijill count, class budget

Nothing is earned by clicking. Two things are scarce, and both are visible to peers.

Phases 1–5 are a few days of work, not a term. Because the mechanics live in shared components, adding the hard lane to the engine adds it to all 114 lessons at once. The long pole is not code — it is writing 114 good hard questions, and phase 3 deliberately defers 107 of them until you know the mechanic works.

6 · The three things most likely to break this

  1. A teacher answers a question without taking a token. Scarcity mechanics die the first time the rule is not held. This is a training issue, not a code issue, and it is the most probable failure.
  2. The Sijill fills up with the same four names. Watch the spread, not the total. If the same confident students are named every week, the hard lane is pitched too high — lower it until the middle of the class can reach it.
  3. Someone re-adds points "to encourage the quiet ones". It will be well meant and it will undo the design. Participation points are exactly what tells a status-conscious child that the currency is worthless.
