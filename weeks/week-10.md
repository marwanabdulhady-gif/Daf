### Essential Question

How can you estimate quotients using patterns and place value?

### Visual Learning Bridge

On “Clean Up Your Town Day,” 1,320 people volunteered to clean up the Springville parks. The volunteers were divided equally into teams to work in each of the town's parks. About how many people were on each team? (6 parks)

Multiplication and division are related. Mentally multiplying by tens or hundreds can help to estimate the quotient of a multi-digit division problem.

**Use Division Facts and Place-Value Patterns**

Find compatible numbers to estimate 1,320 ÷ 6.  
You know 12 ÷ 6 = 2, and 120 ÷ 6 = 20, so 1,200 ÷ 6 = 200.  
1,320 ÷ 6 is about **200**.  
There are about 200 people on each team.

**Use Multiplication Patterns**

Estimate 1,320 ÷ 6.  
6 times what number is about 1,320?  
You know 6 × 2 = 12, and 6 × 20 = 120, so 6 × 200 = 1,200.  
1,200 is close to 1,320.  
There are about **200 people** on each team.

**Convince Me!** Construct Arguments: Complete the calculations 4 × 100, 4 × 200, 4 × 300, 4 × 400. Explain how you can use the calculations to estimate 1,296 ÷ 4.

### Another Example — Rounding

You can use rounding to estimate quotients.

Estimate 357 ÷ 8 by rounding the dividend.  
Round: 400 ÷ 8 = 50  
So, 357 ÷ 8 is about **50**.

Estimate 5,582 ÷ 7 by rounding the dividend.  
Round: 5,600 ÷ 7 = 800  
So, 5,582 ÷ 7 is about **800**.

### Essential Question

After dividing, what do you do with the remainder?

### Visual Learning Bridge

When you divide with whole numbers, any whole number that remains after the division is complete is called the **remainder**.

Nawaf has 27 football cards in an album. He put 6 cards on each page. He knows 27 ÷ 6 = 4 with 3 left over, because 6 × 4 = 24 and 24 + 3 = 27.

Use an **R** to represent the remainder: **27 ÷ 6 = 4 R3**

How do you use the remainder to answer questions about division?

| Question | What to do with the remainder | Answer |
|----------|-------------------------------|--------|
| How many pages did Nawaf **fill**? | Ignore the remainder. Find how many groups of 6 there are. | 4 pages |
| How many pages did Nawaf **work on**? | Because there is a remainder, add 1 to the quotient. | 5 pages |
| How many cards did Nawaf put on the **fifth page**? | The answer is the remainder. | 3 cards |

**Convince Me!** Critique Reasoning: The calculation to the right is incorrect. What error was made? What is the correct answer?  
45 ÷ 6 = 6 R9  *(remainder cannot be ≥ divisor)*

### Another Example

There are 20 apples to arrange in gift baskets, with 6 apples in each basket. How many baskets can be filled? Explain the meaning of the remainder.

3 equal groups of 6 with 2 left over.  
20 ÷ 6 = 3 R2, because 6 × 3 = 18 and 18 + 2 = 20.  
The remainder, 2, represents the number of apples **not** placed into gift baskets.

### Solve & Share

Salman's bird feeder holds 6 cups of bird feed. How many times can Salman's bird feeder be filled using a 72-cup bag of bird feed? Use counters or draw pictures to solve this problem. Explain how you found your answer.

You can think about how many times you can take away groups of six from your original number.

**Look Back!** Reasoning: How can you use multiplication to check your answer?

### Essential Question

How can you use partial quotients to solve division problems?

### Visual Learning Bridge

There are 3 seats in each row of an airplane for passengers. If there are 63 people boarding the airplane, how many rows of seats are needed?

You can divide by finding **partial quotients** until only a remainder, if any, is left.

**One Way**

How many groups of 3 counters can be taken away from 63?

Estimate: How many 3s are in 63? Try 10.  
10 groups of 3 is 30. 63 − 30 = 33  
Estimate: How many 3s are in 33? Try 10.  
10 groups of 3 is 30. 33 − 30 = 3  
How many 3s are in 3? 1  
1 group of 3 is 3. 3 − 3 = 0

Partial quotients: 10 + 10 + 1 = **21**  
There are 21 groups of 3 in 63. **21 rows** of seats are needed.

**Another Way** — Sometimes you can use a different first estimate.

Estimate: How many 3s are in 63? Try 20.  
20 groups of 3 is 60. 63 − 60 = 3  
How many 3s are in 3? 1  
Partial quotients: 20 + 1 = **21**

**Convince Me!** Use Structure: How can you use the relationships between multiplication and division to check your answer?

### Another Example — Distributive Property

Break apart 69 using place value. Use the Distributive Property to show 69 ÷ 3.

```
69 ÷ 3 = (60 + 9) ÷ 3
       = (60 ÷ 3) + (9 ÷ 3)
       = 20 + 3
       = 23
```

## Week 10 Notes

- Remainder rules: remainder must be less than the divisor.
- Interpreting remainders: ignore / add 1 / the remainder itself — depends on the question.
- Partial quotients: any friendly multiples work; more “chunks” is fine as long as they add correctly.
