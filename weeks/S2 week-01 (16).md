### Solve & Share

How many ways can you fold a square so one half fits exactly on the other? How many ways for a given letter (e.g., R or A)? A mirror can help.

**Look Back!** What figures can you form when you fold a square in half? How are they related to symmetry?

### Visual Learning Bridge

A figure is **line symmetric** if it can be folded on a line so both halves match exactly. That fold is a **line of symmetry**.

- A figure can have **no** lines of symmetry (cannot fold to matching parts).
- A figure can have **one** line of symmetry (e.g., the truck picture).
- A figure can have **more than one** (example: 2 lines).
- A figure can have **many** (example: 6 lines).
- A **circle** has infinitely many.

Count the lines drawn on each figure.

**Convince Me!** Two capital letters with exactly one line of symmetry (A, M, T, U, V, W, Y). Two with exactly two (H, I, O, X).

### Practice

Tell if a dashed line is a line of symmetry. How many lines each figure has. Is each figure line symmetric? Draw the lines.

20. Jefferson Memorial — line symmetric? Where? Type of triangle outlined in green.
22. How can you tell a line is **not** a line of symmetry? (Halves don’t match.)
23. **HOT:** How many lines of symmetry can a parallelogram have? *(A non-rhombus, non-rectangle parallelogram: 0. A rectangle: 2. A rhombus: 2. A square: 4.)*
24. Which figure has six lines of symmetry? 25. Which is NOT line symmetric?

### Visual Learning Bridge

Saud sketched **half** of a line-symmetric tabletop. Two ways to complete it:

**One Way:** Draw a line of symmetry. Reflect the design across that line.

**Another Way:** Draw a **different** line of symmetry and complete the opposite side.

The tabletop is line symmetric if the design can be folded along a line of symmetry into matching parts.

## Lesson 16-6 — Critique Reasoning (as time allows, p. 605)

**I can…** critique the reasoning of others using what I know about two-dimensional shapes.

Nader: “All right triangles have two sides the same length.” He showed three isosceles right triangles. **Flaw:** a **scalene** right triangle is a counterexample. One counterexample shows the statement is false.

Ammar: “Every quadrilateral has at least one right angle.” She drew only rectangles/squares. **Flaw:** she used only special cases. A parallelogram that is not a rectangle has no right angles.

## Week 16 Notes

- Fold test: matching parts, not just equal area.
- Square: 4 lines; rectangle (not square): 2; rhombus (not square): 2; general parallelogram: 0; circle: infinitely many.
- One counterexample disproves a “all” statement.
- Hierarchy language: “All squares are rectangles” is true; “all rectangles are squares” is not.
