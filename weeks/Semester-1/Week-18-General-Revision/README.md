## Revision days

### Day 1 — Topic 5: Divide by 1-Digit Numbers

**Must-know**

- Mental quotients using basic facts and place value: 1,800 ÷ 6 = 300.
- Estimate quotients with compatible numbers (and rounding when it helps).
- Interpret remainders: ignore / add 1 / the remainder itself - depends on the question.
- Partial quotients for 2-, 3-, and 4-digit dividends.
- Sharing / place-value blocks; standard recording of division.
- Remainder must be less than the divisor.

**Quick Practice** (6 items, as printed)

1. 2,800 ÷ 7
1. Estimate 700 ÷ 9 and 1,320 ÷ 6
1. 47 students, 4 per car. How many cars? Interpret the remainder.
1. 63 ÷ 3 using partial quotients (try 10 + 10 + 1 and try 20 + 1).
1. 277 ÷ 5. How many group interviews of 5 if 277 people audition? (remainder → extra group)
1. Check: (quotient × divisor) + remainder = dividend.

### Day 2 — Topic 6: Operations to Solve Problems

**Must-know**

- Addition comparison ("more than") vs multiplication comparison ("times as many").
- Inverse: if A is 4 times as far as B, then B = A ÷ 4.
- Hidden questions in multi-step problems.
- Bar diagrams + equations with variables.
- Make sense and persevere: know / plan / try another way / check.

**Quick Practice** (5 items, as printed)

1. Stars scored 4; Rangers scored 3 times as many and 8 more. Could both statements be true?
1. Khalid travels 192 miles, 4 times as far as Salman. How far does Salman travel?
1. 8 buses × 24 students and 6 vans × 15 students. How many fewer third graders?
1. 178 students; 12 rows of 8 fifth graders; 8 per row. How many rows for fourth graders? (interpret remainder)
1. Tuba costs 9 × SAR 159; Ali has SAR 1,025; earns SAR 8/h. How many hours? (50 R6 → 51 hours)

### Day 3 — Topic 7: Factors and Multiples

**Must-know**

- Factor pairs from arrays; stop when pairs repeat.
- Square numbers have an odd number of factors.
- Prime: exactly two factors (1 and itself). Composite: more than two factors. 1 is neither. Only even prime: 2.
- A multiple is the product of a given factor and a whole number.
- Every whole number is a multiple of each of its factors.

**Quick Practice** (6 items, as printed)

1. All factor pairs of 24. All arrays for 12 chairs.
1. Factors of 16, 20, 36. Which are square numbers?
1. Prime or composite: 2, 9, 17, 21, 47, 51, 97.
1. Five multiples of 8. Is 75 a multiple of 8?
1. Why does 16 have an odd number of factors?
1. Khalid's 36 photos: list arrays, factors, factor pairs; prime or composite?

### Day 4 — Topic 8: Fraction Equivalence and Ordering

**Must-know**

- Equivalent fractions: same amount (area model, same point on a number line).
- Generate equivalents: multiply or divide numerator and denominator by the same whole number (n/n = 1).
- Benchmarks: 0, 1/2, 1.
- Compare: common denominator, same numerator, benchmarks, number line.
- Compare only fractions that refer to the same whole.

**Quick Practice** (6 items, as printed)

1. Show 1/2 = 2/4 = 3/6 with an area model and a number line.
1. Generate three fractions equivalent to 2/3 (multiplication).
1. Simplify 8/12 and 18/24.
1. Use a benchmark to compare 3/8 and 5/6.
1. Compare 2/3 and 3/5 with a common denominator.
1. Order: 2/3, 5/8, 3/4.
