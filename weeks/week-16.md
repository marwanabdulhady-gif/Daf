### Solve & Share

Saud bought a piece of ribbon. The length of the ribbon is given in tenths. Write the length as two other equivalent fractions.

**Look Back!** Saud wrote ⁶⁄₁₀ = ⅗. What two operations could Saud have used? Explain.

### Essential Question

How can you use division to find equivalent fractions?

### Visual Learning Bridge

In early May, Fairbanks, the Northern Borders Region, has daylight for ¹⁸⁄₂₄ of the day. What are some fractions equivalent to ¹⁸⁄₂₄?

A **common factor** is a factor two or more numbers have in common.

Two common factors of 18 and 24 are 2 and 3.

To find equivalent fractions, divide the numerator and the denominator by a common factor greater than 1.

```
18 ÷ 2 = 9     18 ÷ 3 = 6
24 ÷ 2 = 12    24 ÷ 3 = 8
```

¹⁸⁄₂₄, ⁹⁄₁₂, and ⁶⁄₈ are equivalent fractions.

¹⁸⁄₂₄ of a day is equal to ⁹⁄₁₂ of a day and to ⁶⁄₈ of a day.

**Convince Me!** Model with Math: Draw a number line and label it with equivalent fractions to show ¹⁰⁄₁₈ = ⁵⁄₉.

### Guided Practice

1. Use division to show ⁹⁄₁₂ and ¾ are equivalent.
2. Is there a fraction with a smaller numerator and denominator equivalent to ⁵⁄₇? Explain.

For 3–8, divide to find equivalent fractions (e.g., ¹⁰⁄₁₂ = ⁵⁄₆; ¹⁰⁰⁄₁₀ = 10).

Leveled Practice For 9–16, fill in missing numbers.

For 17–24, divide to find two equivalent fractions.

### Problem Solving

**Look Back!** Generalize: How could you tell if a fraction is greater than, less than, or equal to ½ just by looking at the numerator and the denominator?

### Essential Question

How can you use benchmarks to compare fractions?

### Visual Learning Bridge

Rashid needs ⅜ stick of butter to make muffins and ⅔ stick of butter to make cookies. Which recipe uses more butter?

You can use **benchmark fractions** to compare fractions. Benchmark fractions are commonly used fractions such as ½, ⅓, and ¼.

You can compare these fractions because they refer to the **same whole** — a stick of butter.

Compare ⅜ to the benchmark ½: ⅜ < ½.  
Compare ⅔ to the benchmark ½: ⅔ > ½.  
So ⅜ < ⅔. The cookie recipe uses more butter.

**Convince Me!** Critique Reasoning: Emad said, “I know ⅛ is less than ⅞ because ⅛ is closer to 0 than to 1 and ⅞ is closer to 1 than to 0.” Does Emad’s reasoning make sense? Draw two number lines.

### Another Example

Compare ⁹⁄₁₀ and ⅚. Use 1 whole as a benchmark. ⁹⁄₁₀ is closer to 1; ⅚ is also close to 1 — then use another strategy if needed.

### Guided Practice

1. Salman found ⅝ is equal to ½? and ⅜ is less than ½. How can Salman compare ⅝ and ⅜?
2. Write a fraction closer to 0 than to 1, and one closer to 1 than to 0. Complete a comparison.

For 3–4, compare. Write <, >, or =.

For 5–10, circle fractions that match: less than ½, greater than ½, greater than 1, closer to 0 than to 1.

For 11–18, compare using benchmarks or 1.

### Solve & Share

You can select tools: drawings, number lines, or fraction strips.

**Look Back!** Karim read for ¼ of an hour. Did Karim read more or less than Jawad? Write a number sentence using >, <, or =.

### Essential Question

How can you compare fractions with unlike numerators and denominators?

### Visual Learning Bridge

Ibrahim’s father is building a model dinosaur. Compare ¼ inch and ⅗ inch. Then compare ½ inch and ⁵⁄₁₀ inch.

You can compare these fractions because they refer to the same whole, an inch.

The fraction that is **farther to the right** on a number line is greater.

**Compare ¼ and ⅗** by renaming so they both have the same denominator.

```
1/4 = (1 × 3)/(4 × 3) = 3/12
3/5 = (3 × 2)? wait: 4 and 5 → LCD 20, or 12 as in the book:
Book uses:
1/4 = 3/12,  5/6 related example.

From the dump:
1 × 3 / 4 × 3 = 3/12
5 × 2 / 6 × 2 = 10/12
3/12 < 10/12 so 1/4 < 5/6
```

When two fractions have the **same numerators**, the fraction with the **smaller denominator** is greater (larger pieces).

**Convince Me!** Critique Reasoning: Khalil said, “These are easy to compare. I just think about ½.” Circle the greater of ⅝ and ⁵⁄₈-related fractions. Explain what Khalil was thinking.

### Another Example

Compare ⅗ and ⁶⁄₁₀. Create an equivalent so numerators are the same: ⁶⁄₁₀ ÷ 2/2 = ⅗. Equal.

When two fractions have the same numerators, the fraction with the smaller denominator is greater: ¾ > ⅗ because fourths are larger pieces than fifths.

### Solve & Share

Sultan and Karim started with small bottles filled with water. Tareq started with a larger bottle that was ¾ full. At the end, Sultan and Tareq’s bottles were each half filled. Karim’s bottle was ⅔ filled. Who has the most water left? Construct a math argument.

**Thinking Habits:** How can I use numbers, objects, drawings, or actions to justify my argument? Am I using numbers and symbols correctly? Is my explanation clear and complete?

**Key idea:** ½ of a **larger** whole is more than ½ of a smaller whole. Equivalent fractions only represent the same amount when the wholes are the same size.

Emad said ½ is the same amount as ²⁄₄. Majid said ½ and ²⁄₄ can be different amounts. **Both students are correct**, depending on whether the wholes are the same size.

**Look Back!** If Tareq’s bottle was ⅝ filled at the end, would you be able to decide who had the most water left?
