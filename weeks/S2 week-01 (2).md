**I can…** use division to find equivalent fractions.

Fairbanks, the Northern Borders Region: daylight ¹⁸⁄₂₄ of the day.

Common factors of 18 and 24: 2 and 3.

```
18 ÷ 2 = 9 / 24 ÷ 2 = 12    →  9/12
18 ÷ 3 = 6 / 24 ÷ 3 = 8     →  6/8
```

¹⁸⁄₂₄ = ⁹⁄₁₂ = ⁶⁄₈.

**Look Back:** ⁶⁄₁₀ = ⅗ could be division by 2, or multiplication the other way.

If numerator and denominator are both odd, you may still simplify (e.g., ⁹⁄₁₅ = ⅗) or you may not (e.g., ⁵⁄₇).

**I can…** use benchmarks, area models, and number lines to compare fractions.

**Benchmark fractions:** ½, ⅓, ¼ (and 0, 1).

Rashid: ⅜ stick of butter for muffins, ⅔ for cookies. Same whole.

⅜ < ½ < ⅔ ⇒ cookies use more butter.

Generalize: compare numerator to half the denominator to see if a fraction is >, <, or = ½.

Closer to 0 than to 1: numerator much smaller than denominator (e.g., ¹⁄₁₀). Closer to 1: numerator close to denominator (e.g., ⁹⁄₁₀).
