### Solve & Share

Jawad used 1⅓ cups apple juice and ⅞? cups orange juice. How much juice in all?

**Look Back!** Could you find 1⅓ + ⅞ by adding (1+1) + (⅓+⅞)? Explain.  
Generalize from adding fractions.

### Visual Learning Bridge

Badr mixes sand with 2⅜ cups of potting mixture. After mixing, how many cups of soil?

You can use **Commutative and Associative Properties**. Breaking apart a mixed number is using those properties.

**Way 1 — break apart**

```
2 3/8 + 1 7/8
= (2 + 1) + (3/8 + 7/8)
= 3 + 10/8
= 3 + 1 2/8
= 4 2/8
```

**Way 2 — improper fractions**

```
2 3/8 = 19/8,  1 7/8 = 15/8
19/8 + 15/8 = 34/8 = 4 2/8
```

**Convince Me!** How is adding mixed numbers like adding fractions and whole numbers?

### Solve & Share

Emad walks 2⅚ miles to his aunt’s. He has walked ⅚ mile. How much farther?

**Look Back!** You found 2⅚ − ⅚ = *m*. Write a related addition equation.

### Visual Learning Bridge

Tennis ball 2⅝ inches across; golf ball 1⅝? (dump: golf 1 ⁷⁄₆ related). Difference?

**Subtract mixed numbers** (regroup if needed):

```
2 3/6 − 1 5/6
Rename 2 3/6 = 1 9/6   (because 1 = 6/6)
1 9/6 − 1 5/6 = 4/6
```

**Subtract equivalent (improper) fractions:**

```
2 3/6 = 15/6,  1 5/6 = 11/6
15/6 − 11/6 = 4/6
```

Count up to check: 1 ⁵⁄₆ + ⁴⁄₆ = 2 ³⁄₆.

The tennis ball is ⅔ inch wider than the golf ball (if ⁴⁄₆ = ⅔).

**Convince Me!** Why rename 4⅛ to subtract 4⅛ − ⅞?

### Solve & Share

Majed wrote ⅘ = ⅕ + ⅕ + ⅕ + ⅕. Khalil said she could use multiplication. Is Khalil correct?

**Look Back!** Write an equation relating Majed’s work and Khalil’s observation.

### Visual Learning Bridge

A **unit fraction** describes one part of the whole; numerator is always 1.

Khalid ran ¾ of the way to school. Describe ¾ using unit fractions.

When a whole is divided into four equal parts, each part is ¼.

```
1/4 + 1/4 + 1/4 = 3 × (1/4) = 3/4
```

Just as 6 + 6 + 6 = 3 × 6,  
¼ + ¼ + ¼ = 3 × ¼.

So ¾ is a **multiple** of ¼.

**Convince Me!** ⅝ is a multiple of what unit fraction?

### Practice

Write each fraction as a multiple of a unit fraction: ⅔ = 2 × ⅓; ⅚ = 5 × ⅙; ⅞; ⁴⁄₆; etc.
