### Essential Question

How can you model and solve multi-step problems?

### Visual Learning Bridge

A school uses 8 buses to transport the fourth-grade students and 6 vans to transport the third-grade students on a field trip. Each bus holds 24 students. Each van holds 15 students. How many fewer third-grade students than fourth-grade students are on the field trip?

You can use variables or letters to represent unknown quantities.

**Step 1 — Find and answer the hidden questions.**

Hidden Question: How many fourth-grade students are on the field trip?  
*F* = Number of fourth graders  
*F* = 8 × 24 = **192** fourth-grade students

Hidden Question: How many third-grade students are on the field trip?  
*T* = Number of third graders  
*T* = 6 × 15 = **90** third-grade students

**Step 2 — Use the answers to the hidden questions to answer the original question.**

Original Question: How many fewer third-grade students than fourth-grade students are on the field trip?  
*S* = the number fewer  
*S* = 192 − 90 = **102**

**102 fewer** third-grade students than fourth-grade students are on the field trip.

**Convince Me!** Construct Arguments: Ammar says she can solve the problem using the equation *S* = (8 × 24) − (6 × 15). Do you agree? Explain.

### Another Example

The Al-Harbi family gets 2 pizzas and 4 shawarma wraps at Eat-It-Up. What is the total cost?

Pizza SAR 18 · Shawarma SAR 6 · Chicken burger SAR 3 · Salad SAR 10 · Turkey Wrap SAR 9 · Drinks SAR 2

2 × 18 and 4 × 6 are **expressions**.  
*T* = (2 × 18) + (4 × 6) is an **equation**.

**Step 1** Write expressions to represent the hidden questions.  
What is the cost of 2 pizzas?  2 × 18  
What is the cost of 4 shawarma wraps?  4 × 6

**Step 2** Combine the expressions into an equation to find *T*, the total cost.  
*T* = (2 × 18) + (4 × 6)

**Step 3** Solve.  
*T* = 36 + 24 = **60**  
The total cost is **SAR 60**.

### Visual Learning Bridge

The students in the fourth and fifth grades are going to a concert. There are 178 students. There are 12 rows of 8 fifth graders. How many rows are needed for the fourth graders? (8 students per row)

To find the number of rows, you need to find the number of fourth graders. To find the number of fourth graders, you need to find the number of fifth graders.

Let *s* = the number of fifth graders, *f* = the number of fourth graders, and *r* = the number of rows of fourth graders.

**Step 1 — Find and solve the first hidden question.**  
How many fifth graders are there?  
12 × 8 = *s*  
*s* = **96**  
There are 96 fifth graders.

**Step 2 — Find and solve the second hidden question.**  
How many fourth graders are there?  
178 − 96 = *f*  
*f* = **82**  
There are 82 fourth graders.

**Step 3 — Answer the original question.**  
How many rows are needed for the fourth graders?  
82 ÷ 8 = *r*  
*r* = **10 R2**

Ten rows will be filled with 2 fourth graders left over. So, **11 rows** will be needed.

**Convince Me!** Construct Arguments: Does the answer of 11 rows make sense for the problem above? Explain.

### Essential Question

How can you make sense of and persevere in solving a problem?

### Visual Learning Bridge

Badr and Ali have to buy their own instruments for the band. Ali's tuba costs 9 times as much as Badr's trumpet. Badr's trumpet costs SAR 159. Ali made SAR 1,025 from a fundraiser. He has a part-time job that pays SAR 8 an hour. How many hours does Ali need to work to have enough money to buy his instrument?

**How can I make sense of and solve this problem?**

I can
- identify the quantities given.
- understand how the quantities are related.
- choose and implement an appropriate strategy.
- check to be sure my work and answer make sense.

**What do you need to do to solve this problem?**  
I need to determine how much Ali's tuba costs and how much Ali needs to earn to buy the tuba.

Use the cost of the trumpet to find the cost of the tuba.  
SAR 159 × 9 = **SAR 1,431**  Ali's tuba costs SAR 1,431.

Find how much more money Ali needs to earn.  
SAR 1,431 − SAR 1,025 = **SAR 406**  Ali needs to earn SAR 406.

Find how many hours Ali needs to work.  
SAR 406 ÷ 8 = **50 R6**

Ali will not have enough money by working 50 hours, so he needs to work **51 hours**.

**Convince Me!** Make Sense and Persevere: How can you check to make sure the work and answer given above make sense?
