### Solve & Share

A number line is an example of a line (goes on forever both ways). Draw: two lines that never cross; two that cross at one point; two that cross at two points. If you cannot, tell why.

**Look Back!** Turki said, “The lines shown intersect at three points.” Correct?

Two distinct lines intersect at **at most one point**. They cannot cross at two points.

### Visual Learning Bridge

A **line** is a straight path of points that goes on and on in opposite directions.

| Pair | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| **Parallel lines** | Never intersect |
| **Intersecting lines** | Pass through the same point |
| **Perpendicular lines** | Intersect to form **right angles** |

Perpendicular lines are intersecting, but intersecting lines are not always perpendicular. Intersecting lines are not parallel.

Railroad tracks are **parallel**; ties are **perpendicular** to the tracks.

**Convince Me!** Classroom examples of parallel, intersecting, and perpendicular lines.

### Practice

Name points, lines, parallel pairs, perpendicular pairs. Draw each type.

17. If all perpendicular lines are intersecting, are all intersecting lines perpendicular? **No.**
18. Draw three lines: two perpendicular, third intersecting them at exactly one point.
19. **HOT:** AB ∥ CD, CD ⊥ EF. Line through B and D ⊥ AB. Relationship between BD and EF?

### Solve & Share

Sort given triangles into two or more groups. Explain.

**Look Back!** What is true about all 7 triangles you sorted?

### Visual Learning Bridge

**By sides**

- **Equilateral** — 3 sides the same length
- **Isosceles** — at least 2 sides the same length
- **Scalene** — no sides the same length

**By angles**

- **Acute triangle** — three acute angles
- **Right triangle** — one right angle
- **Obtuse triangle** — one obtuse angle

Triangles can be classified by **both** (e.g., obtuse scalene).

**Convince Me!** Can a triangle have more than one obtuse angle? *(No — the three angles sum to 180°.)* More than one right angle? No.

### Another Example

Pattern: right, acute, right, acute, … and isosceles, scalene, scalene, isosceles, scalene, … Next: acute scalene.

### Practice

Classify by sides then angles. Equilateral backyard — what about the other two sides? Rabbit field of vision — classify the triangle. Pattern fifth shape. **HOT:** One obtuse angle — all possible classifications (obtuse scalene or obtuse isosceles; cannot be equilateral or right).

### Solve & Share

Draw three different four-sided shapes with opposite sides parallel. Alike? Different?

**Look Back!** What attributes do your shapes have in common?

### Visual Learning Bridge

A **quadrilateral** is a polygon with four sides.

| Figure | Properties |
|--------|------------|
| **Trapezoid** | Only **one** pair of parallel sides *(enVision definition)* |
| **Parallelogram** | Two pairs of parallel sides |
| **Rectangle** | 4 right angles (also a parallelogram) |
| **Rhombus** | All sides equal; opposite sides parallel (also a parallelogram) |
| **Square** | 4 right angles and all sides equal (parallelogram, rectangle, **and** rhombus) |

Trapezoids: only one pair of parallel sides. Parallelograms, rectangles, squares, rhombuses: two pairs.

**Convince Me!** How are a parallelogram and a rectangle the same? Different?

### Another Example

A trapezoid **can** have two right angles (right trapezoid) — perpendicular sides.

## Week 15 Notes

- Two distinct lines meet at 0 or 1 point — never 2.
- Triangle angle sum 180° ⇒ at most one right or obtuse angle.
- Square ⊂ rectangle ⊂ parallelogram ⊂ quadrilateral; square ⊂ rhombus ⊂ parallelogram. Trapezoid (this program) is **not** a parallelogram.
