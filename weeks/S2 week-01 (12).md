### Solve & Share

Paint covers 168 sq ft of a wall 8 ft high. Tape along top, bottom, and sides. Width of the wall? How much tape?

**Look Back!** Describe the steps.

### Visual Learning Bridge

State park perimeter 36 miles; width 7 miles. What is the area?

**Formulas**

- Perimeter: *P* = (2 × ℓ) + (2 × *w*)
- Area: *A* = ℓ × *w*

Opposite sides of a rectangle are equal.

**Step 1 — find length**

2 × 7 = 14  
36 − 14 = 22  (two lengths)  
22 ÷ 2 = **11 miles**

**Step 2 — area**

*A* = 11 × 7 = **77 square miles**

**Convince Me!** Another park area 216 sq mi, width 8 mi. Length? Perimeter?

### Practice

Unknown side given *P* or *A*. Sandbox area 16 sq ft, whole-number sides: possible dimensions.

10. Picture frame *P* = 50½ in. Width?
11. Felt on the back: picture ½ in. shorter and 1 in. less wide. Area of felt?
13. Tabletop *A* = 18 sq ft and *P* = 18 ft. Dimensions?
14. Ammar 6-ft fence vs Zayd 8-ft fence, both 24 ft of fencing. Whose garden greater area?
15. **HOT:** Runner area 80 sq in.; length 5 times width; whole numbers. Dimensions?
16. Rectangle area 144 sq cm, one side 8 cm. Perimeter?

### Solve & Share

Start 18, add 3 — next 6 numbers. Start 17, add 2. Start __, subtract 4. Features of each pattern (even/odd/alternating).

**Look Back!** Two patterns, same rule, different starts. Feature of each.

### Visual Learning Bridge

House numbers follow “Add 4.” Next three? A feature?

A **rule** is a mathematical phrase that tells how numbers or shapes in a pattern are related.

Some patterns have **features not given in the rule** (all even; all multiples of 5; alternating even/odd).

Number line: 16, 20, 24, 28, … all even.

**Convince Me!** Can “Add 4” create a pattern of all odd numbers? *(Yes, if you start with an odd number.)*

### Another Example

“Subtract 5” after 825: 820, 815, 810. All multiples of 5.

### Practice

Continue: Subtract 6 from 48; Subtract 3 from 63; Add 7 from 444; Add 25; Subtract 4 from 75. Generate from rules: Subtract 10 from 90; Add 51 from 16; etc.

### Solve & Share

6 juice boxes in 1 pack, 12 in 2, 18 in 3. Complete the table (×6). How many in 10 packs? 100 packs? Features.

**Look Back!** Table: bicycles → wheels, rule “Multiply by 2.” Five rows. Features.

### Visual Learning Bridge

Cloverleaf: 3 leaflets each.

| Cloverleaves | Leaflets |
|--------------|----------|
| 1 | 3 |
| 2 | 6 |
| 3 | 9 |
| 4 | 12 |

Rule leaflets: **Multiply by 3**. Rule cloverleaves given leaflets: **Divide by 3**.

Number of leaflets is a **multiple** of the number of cloverleaves. Number of cloverleaves is a **factor** of the number of leaflets.

**Convince Me!** Expression for leaflets given cloverleaves *c*? For cloverleaves given leaflets?

### Practice

Tables: multiply by 8 (spider legs); divide by 4 (fingers/hands); multiply by 45 (Yusuf painting); divide by 8 (potato bags); batteries ×3; rolls ÷6.
