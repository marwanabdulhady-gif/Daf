### Essential Question

How can you use an area model and partial products to multiply?

### Visual Learning Bridge

A garden is in the shape of a rectangle. It is 8 feet wide and 25 feet long. What is the area of the garden?

A **numerical expression** contains numbers and at least one operation. 8 × 25 is a numerical expression.

You can use a **rectangular area model** to show multiplication. The product of 8 × 25 is the area of the rectangle.

The **Distributive Property** says that multiplying a sum by a number is the same as multiplying each part of the sum by that number and adding the partial products.

```
8 × 25 = (8 × 20) + (8 × 5)
```

**What You Show**

```
        20          5
    ┌──────────┬─────┐
  8 │  8 × 20  │ 8×5 │
    │   160    │  40 │
    └──────────┴─────┘
```

160 + 40 = 200

**What You Record**

```
   25
 ×  8
   40     8 × 5 ones
+ 160     8 × 2 tens
  200
```

The area of the garden is **200 square feet**.

**Estimate:** 8 × 25 is about 8 × 30 = 240. The product, 200, is close to the estimate of 240. The answer is reasonable.

**Convince Me!** Use Structure: Does 12 − (4 × 2) = (12 − 4) × (12 − 2)? Explain.

### Another Example

Find 5 × 123.

```
        100         20        3
    ┌──────────┬─────────┬─────┐
  5 │ 5 × 100  │ 5 × 20  │ 5×3 │
    │   500    │   100   │  15 │
    └──────────┴─────────┴─────┘
```

```
   123
 ×   5
    15
   100
+  500
   615
```

You can use what you know about multiplying 2-digit numbers to multiply 3-digit numbers.

### Solve & Share

You can use structure to multiply the numbers in any order and make the computations easier.

**Look Back!** Describe two different ways to find 4 × 97.

### Essential Question

How can you multiply mentally?

### Visual Learning Bridge

Three cyclists rode their bikes the distances shown in the table. Use mental math to calculate the total distance Pam and Anas each rode.

| Cyclist | Distance |
|---------|----------|
| Pam | 325 miles a month for 4 months |
| Anas | 25 miles a week for 8 weeks |
| Ghazi | 398 miles a month for 3 months |

You can use properties of operations to help multiply mentally. According to the **Commutative Property of Multiplication**, you can multiply in any order.

**Multiply 4 × 325** to find the distance Pam rode.

To multiply mentally, you can break apart numbers using place value and the **Distributive Property**.

```
4 × 325 = 4 × 300 + 4 × 25
        = 1,200 + 100
        = 1,300
```

Pam rode **1,300 miles**.

**Multiply 8 × 25** to find the distance Anas rode.

To multiply mentally, you can break apart and rearrange numbers using the Commutative and Associative Properties.

```
8 × 25 = (4 × 2) × 25
       = 2 × (4 × 25)
       = 2 × 100
       = 200
```

Anas rode **200 miles**.

**Convince Me!** Use Structure: In the work for 4 × 325 above, why was 25 not broken into 20 and 5? Explain.

### Another Example — Compensation

Determine the distance Ghazi rode. Find 3 × 398.

400 is close to 398. Find 3 × 400 and adjust the answer.

3 × 400 = 1,200  
398 + 2 = 400, so 3 × 2 = 6  
Adjust the answer by subtracting 6.  
1,200 − 6 = 1,194

Ghazi rode **1,194 miles** in 3 months.

Some mental math strategies include both compensation and properties of operations.
