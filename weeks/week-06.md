### Essential Question

How can you multiply by multiples of 10, 100, and 1,000?

### Visual Learning Bridge

Calculate 3 × 50, 3 × 500, and 3 × 5,000 using basic multiplication facts and properties of operations.

**One Way — Basic facts and place value**

```
3 × 50   = 3 × 5 tens     = 15 tens     = 150
3 × 500  = 3 × 5 hundreds = 15 hundreds = 1,500
3 × 5,000 = 3 × 5 thousands = 15 thousands = 15,000
```

**Another Way — Break apart numbers. Use the Associative Property of Multiplication.**

The Associative Property of Multiplication states that you can change the grouping of the factors and the product stays the same.

```
3 × 50   = 3 × (5 × 10)     = (3 × 5) × 10     = 15 × 10     = 150
3 × 500  = 3 × (5 × 100)    = (3 × 5) × 100    = 15 × 100    = 1,500
3 × 5,000 = 3 × (5 × 1,000) = (3 × 5) × 1,000  = 15 × 1,000  = 15,000
```

**Convince Me!** Reasoning: What patterns do you see in the number of zeros in the products above?

### Another Example

Use place value to calculate 5 × 400 and 6 × 5,000.

```
5 × 400   = 5 × 4 hundreds  = 20 hundreds  = 2,000
6 × 5,000 = 6 × 5 thousands = 30 thousands = 30,000
```

If the product of the basic fact ends in zero, the product has one more zero than you see in the factors.

### Essential Question

How can you estimate products?

### Visual Learning Bridge

Ustadh Hatem's class sold calendars and note pads for 3 weeks as a class fundraiser. About how much did Ustadh Hatem's class make selling calendars? selling note pads?

| Item | Week 1 | Week 2 | Week 3 | Price |
|------|--------|--------|--------|-------|
| Calendar | 28 | 73 | 63 | SAR 4 |
| Note Pads | 272 | 475 | 232 | SAR 3 |

**Use compatible numbers** to estimate how many calendars *c* were sold.  
28 + 73 + 63 = *c* → 164 = *c*  
164 is close to 150. 4 × 150 = 600  
Ustadh Hatem's class made about **SAR 600** selling calendars.

**Use rounding** to estimate how many note pads *n* were sold.  
272 + 475 + 232 = *n* → 979 = *n*  
To the nearest thousand, 979 rounds to 1,000.  
3 × 1,000 = 3,000  
Ustadh Hatem's class made about **SAR 3,000** selling note pads.

**Convince Me!** Construct Arguments: Could you solve the problem above by rounding to estimate the total number of calendars and the total number of note pads? Explain.

### Another Example

Ustadha Laila's class made SAR 2,089 selling neon pencil packs. A student calculated that Ustadh Hatem's class and Ustadha Laila's class together made SAR 8,865 selling the school supplies. Is the student's calculation reasonable?

Round Ustadha Laila's total to the nearest thousand: SAR 2,000. Then add the rounded amounts. SAR 2,000 + SAR 600 + SAR 3,000 = SAR 5,600.

The student's calculation is not reasonable because SAR 8,865 is not close to SAR 5,600.

You can estimate to check if an answer is reasonable.

### Solve & Share

A music room has chairs arranged in rows. There are 6 rows. Each row has 18 chairs. How many chairs are in the music room? Solve this problem any way you choose. Explain your answer.

You can model with math. You can represent how the chairs are arranged to help solve the problem.

**Look Back!** Would the answer be different if there were 18 rows with 6 chairs in each row? Explain.

### Essential Question

How can you use an array and partial products to multiply?

### Visual Learning Bridge

The windows of a building on Tahlia Street are in an array. There are 5 rows with 13 windows in each row. How many windows are in this array?

An **array** is an arrangement of objects in equal rows.

You can use place value to break factors apart and the **Distributive Property** to find **partial products**.

Find 5 × 13.  
**Estimate:** 5 × 13 is about 5 × 10 = 50.

```
5 rows, 1 ten in each    5 × 10 = 50
5 rows, 3 ones in each   5 ×  3 = 15
50 + 15 = 65
```

```
   13
 ×  5
   15     5 × 3 ones
+  50     5 × 1 ten
   65
```

The numbers 15 and 50 are called **partial products**. 65 is the product.

There are **65 windows** in the array. The product, 65, is close to the estimate of 50. The answer is reasonable.

**Convince Me!** Use Structure: How are the partial products represented with the place-value blocks?

### Another Example

Find 2 × 126.  
**Estimate:** 2 × 126 is about 2 × 100 = 200.

```
2 × 1 hundred = 200
2 × 2 tens    =  40
2 × 6 ones    =  12
200 + 40 + 12 = 252
```

You can find partial products for hundreds, tens, and ones.
