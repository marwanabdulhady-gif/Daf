### Solve & Share

Rule “Square, Triangle.” What is the 37th shape?

**Look Back!** When the pattern has 37 shapes, how many are triangles?

### Visual Learning Bridge

Rashid: “Triangle, Square, Trapezoid.” What is the 49th shape?

A **repeating pattern** is made of shapes or numbers that form a part that repeats (the core).

Positions:
- Triangle: 1st, 4th, 7th → 1 more than a multiple of 3
- Square: 2nd, 5th, 8th → 1 less than a multiple of 3
- Trapezoid: 3rd, 6th, 9th → multiples of 3

You divide by 3 because there are **3 items** in the repeating pattern.

```
49 ÷ 3 = 16 R1
```

Pattern repeats 16 times; remainder 1 → **1st shape = triangle**.

**Convince Me!** Rule “Square, Triangle, Square, Trapezoid.” 26th shape? Be precise.

### Another Example

Rule 1, 3, 5, 7. 100th number: 4 items in core. 100 ÷ 4 = 25 R0 → last item in the core = **7**.

### Solve & Share

A right angle forms a square corner. Find examples in the classroom. Draw two angles open **less** than a right angle.

**Look Back!** Draw an angle open **more** than a right angle.

### Visual Learning Bridge

| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| **Point** | Exact location in space |
| **Line** | Straight path of points going on in opposite directions |
| **Line segment** | Part of a line with two endpoints |
| **Ray** | Part of a line with one endpoint, continuing forever in one direction |
| **Angle** | Two rays with the same endpoint (**vertex**) |

Angles are named with 3 letters; the vertex is the **center** letter (∠ABC). A ray is named with its endpoint first.

| Angle | What you see |
|-------|----------------|
| **Right** | Square corner (90°) |
| **Acute** | Open less than a right angle |
| **Obtuse** | Open more than a right angle but less than a straight angle |
| **Straight** | Forms a straight line (180°) |

**Convince Me!** Complete figures to show given angles.

### Practice

### Solve & Share

Clock at 3 o’clock. How could you describe the smaller angle the hands make?

**Look Back!** At 3 o’clock, what two fractions do the hands divide the clock into?

### Visual Learning Bridge

An angle is measured in **degrees**. A full circle = **360°**.

A **unit angle** turns through ¹⁄₃₆₀ of a circle and measures **1°**.

Right angles divide a circle into 4 equal parts: 360° ÷ 4 = **90°**.

Angle through ⅙ of a circle: 360° ÷ 6 = **60°**.  
⅔ of a circle: 60° + 60° = **120°** (or 2 × 60°).

You measure length in inches, area in square halalahimeters, capacity in ounces, and angles in **degrees**.

**Convince Me!** Susan thinks measure of angle B > angle A (maybe because rays look longer). Agree? *(Angle measure does not depend on ray length.)*
