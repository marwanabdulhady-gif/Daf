### Solve & Share

Emad plotted lengths of 12 fish. What was the longest fish? The shortest?

Be precise; use appropriate labels.

**Look Back!** What other observations can you make?

### Visual Learning Bridge

A **line plot** shows data along a number line. Each dot (or X) above a point represents one number in the data set.

The numbers along the bottom are the **scale**.

Emad walked his dog (miles): data for 7 days. Line plot from ½ to 3.

- Most dots above 1 → most common distance is **1 mile**.
- Longest: **3 miles**. Shortest: **½ mile**.
- Difference: 3 − ½ = 2½ miles.

**Convince Me!** Write and solve an equation for *m*, total miles Emad walked in 7 days.

### Visual Learning Bridge

Sultan’s colored-pencil lengths (4 to 5 inches by eighths).

**Making a Line Plot**

**Step 1** Draw a number line. Choose a scale from least to greatest. Majid halves, fourths, eighths as needed.

**Step 2** Title the plot. Label what the numbers represent (include units).

**Step 3** Draw a dot (or X) for each data value.

**Convince Me!** Equation for *d*, difference between Sultan’s two shortest pencils.

### Solve & Share

### Visual Learning Bridge

Ammar filled 20 water balloons; Badr 15. Line plots of weights.

- Who filled more? How many more? 20 − 15 = **5 more**.
- Ammar’s heaviest 2⅞ lb; Badr’s 2⅛ lb. Difference: 2⅞ − 2⅛ = **¾ pound**.

Each dot = 1 balloon. Farthest dot to the right = heaviest.

**Convince Me!** How much heavier was Ammar’s heaviest than her lightest? Badr’s heaviest vs lightest? Equations.

### Another Example

Snowfall 5 days. Total inches: add the values (with multiplicity). Most often: value with the most dots.

Use Commutative and Associative Properties to make adding easier.
