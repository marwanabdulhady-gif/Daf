## Day 1 — Topic 5: Divide by 1-Digit Numbers

### Must-know

- Mental quotients using basic facts and place value: 1,800 ÷ 6 = 300.
- Estimate quotients with **compatible numbers** (and rounding when it helps).
- **Interpret remainders:** ignore / add 1 / the remainder itself — depends on the question.
- **Partial quotients** for 2-, 3-, and 4-digit dividends.
- Sharing / place-value blocks; standard recording of division.
- Remainder must be **less than** the divisor.

### Quick Practice

1. 2,800 ÷ 7
2. Estimate 700 ÷ 9 and 1,320 ÷ 6
3. 47 students, 4 per car. How many cars? Interpret the remainder.
4. 63 ÷ 3 using partial quotients (try 10 + 10 + 1 and try 20 + 1).
5. 277 ÷ 5. How many group interviews of 5 if 277 people audition? (remainder → extra group)
6. Check: (quotient × divisor) + remainder = dividend.

### Vocabulary check

dividend · divisor · quotient · remainder · partial quotients · compatible numbers

## Day 2 — Topic 6: Operations to Solve Problems

### Must-know

- **Addition comparison** (“more than”) vs **multiplication comparison** (“times as many”).
- Inverse: if *A* is 4 times as far as *B*, then *B* = *A* ÷ 4.
- **Hidden questions** in multi-step problems.
- Bar diagrams + equations with variables.
- Make sense and persevere: know / plan / try another way / check.

### Quick Practice

1. Stars scored 4; Rangers scored 3 times as many **and** 8 more. Could both statements be true?
2. Khalid travels 192 miles, 4 times as far as Salman. How far does Salman travel?
3. 8 buses × 24 students and 6 vans × 15 students. How many fewer third graders?
4. 178 students; 12 rows of 8 fifth graders; 8 per row. How many rows for fourth graders? (interpret remainder)
5. Tuba costs 9 × SAR 159; Ali has SAR 1,025; earns SAR 8/h. How many hours? (50 R6 → 51 hours)

### Vocabulary check

equation · variable · expression · multiplication comparison · addition comparison

## Day 3 — Topic 7: Factors and Multiples

### Must-know

- **Factor pairs** from arrays; stop when pairs repeat.
- Square numbers have an **odd** number of factors.
- **Prime:** exactly two factors (1 and itself). **Composite:** more than two factors. **1 is neither.** Only even prime: **2**.
- A **multiple** is the product of a given factor and a whole number.
- Every whole number is a multiple of each of its factors.

### Quick Practice

1. All factor pairs of 24. All arrays for 12 chairs.
2. Factors of 16, 20, 36. Which are square numbers?
3. Prime or composite: 2, 9, 17, 21, 47, 51, 97.
4. Five multiples of 8. Is 75 a multiple of 8?
5. Why does 16 have an odd number of factors?
6. Khalid’s 36 photos: list arrays, factors, factor pairs; prime or composite?

### Vocabulary check

factor · factor pair · multiple · prime number · composite number · array · generalization

## Day 4 — Topic 8: Fraction Equivalence and Ordering

### Must-know

- Equivalent fractions: same amount (area model, same point on a number line).
- Generate equivalents: multiply or divide numerator **and** denominator by the same whole number (*n/n* = 1).
- **Benchmarks:** 0, 1/2, 1.
- Compare: common denominator, same numerator, benchmarks, number line.
- Compare only fractions that refer to the **same whole**.

### Quick Practice

1. Show 1/2 = 2/4 = 3/6 with an area model and a number line.
2. Generate three fractions equivalent to 2/3 (multiplication).
3. Simplify 8/12 and 18/24.
4. Use a benchmark to compare 3/8 and 5/6.
5. Compare 2/3 and 3/5 with a common denominator.
6. Order: 2/3, 5/8, 3/4.

### Vocabulary check

equivalent fractions · benchmark fraction · common denominator · common factor · numerator · denominator · unit fraction

## Day 5 — Mixed Problems & Exam Skills

### Multi-topic items

1. Write 160,060 in expanded form. Round to the nearest ten thousand.
2. 30,283 + 63,423 + 6,538. Is the sum reasonable?
3. 4 × 1,842 using an area model (partial products).
4. 25 × 15 four partial products. Estimate first.
5. 1,968 ÷ 6 partial quotients.
6. “Times as many” bar diagram: 9 × 27 artists.
7. Factor pairs of 48 with constraints (≥ 3 rows, ≥ 3 in each row).
8. Is 4/5 > 3/4? Construct an argument with equivalent fractions.

### Exam habits

- Estimate first; check reasonableness after.
- Label variables; tell what they represent.
- Remainder: re-read the question before deciding what to do with it.
- Show work: area models, bar diagrams, partial products/quotients.
- Units in the answer (square feet, hours, rows, …).
